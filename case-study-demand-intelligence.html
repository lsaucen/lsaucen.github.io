What route travelers actually want: demand intelligence from 7.1M conversations

A tourism route was being designed with no direct market data. Instead of guessing, I built a Python pipeline that listens to what travelers of comparable routes worldwide say spontaneously, and turned it into design evidence.

01 · MethodHow the corpus is read

Public conversations from 16 traveler communities were collected and classified against a deductive codebook: motivations, barriers, frictions, services, and life-event triggers, each mapped to traveler profiles (pilgrim, adventurer, solo traveler, explorer) and three macro-groups (pilgrimage, hiking & adventure, independent tourism). Because communities differ enormously in size, everything is measured as prevalence: the share of each profile's conversation that touches a topic, never raw volume.

Methodological guardrail: conversation volume is never read as demand. Prevalence describes what matters to travelers who already do these routes; converting that into market size requires separate demand-side data. The pipeline is explicit about this boundary, and every figure is auditable back to the original text.

02 · Who travels and whyThree macro-groups, three different trips

Motivations separate cleanly. Pilgrimage conversation is dominated by spirituality; hiking & adventure by nature; independent tourism by gastronomy and culture. The same route means a fundamentally different product for each group.

03 · PainsWhere each trip hurts

Friction intensity is profile-specific. Health dominates pilgrimage conversation (blisters, joint injury), safety and management dominate independent tourism, and adventurers concentrate on wildlife and technical risk. The temporal cut shows physical wear rising between 2023 and 2025.

04 · DemandWhat they ask for, where they sleep, what they pay

Service demand follows the trip's logic: lodging, gear, and access transport lead overall, but the ranking reorders sharply by macro-group. Lodging conversation splits by profile (albergues for pilgrims, hostels for solo travelers, camping for adventurers), and observed price co-mentions give a first read on willingness to pay by category.

05 · Trends and formatWhat is rising, and how they want it delivered

Between 2023 and 2025, community, pre-trip planning and camping gain share of conversation while hotels, restaurants and generic gastronomy lose it. On delivery format, organized-tour demand concentrates in solo travelers and explorers, and sentiment shows a clear pattern: travelers love the guided format and merely tolerate the intermediary.

06 · LimitationsWhat this analysis does not claim

The corpus is predominantly anglophone, so it describes the international traveler; local demand is treated separately. Prevalence measures attention, not purchase intent. And findings describe travelers of comparable routes worldwide, not any single destination: the value is in transferring behavioral patterns into design requirements, with every inference traceable to source text.
